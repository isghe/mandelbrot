WGSL double-single portability probe

Open this file directly in a WebGPU-capable browser. No server, no build step.

What this measures

Deep-zoom fractal rendering needs more precision than f32 gives, and WebGPU has no f64. The usual answer is double-single (Dekker) arithmetic: a number is carried as a vec2<f32> of a high and a low part, and the low part is recovered with expressions such as e = b - (s - a). Those expressions are algebraically zero. They only carry information because IEEE-754 rounding makes them non-zero, so they survive only if the compiler does not reassociate or simplify them.

WGSL explicitly permits that simplification: §15.7.5 Reassociation and Fusion says an implementation "may reassociate and/or fuse operations if the optimization is at least as accurate as the original formulation", and WebGPU currently offers no way to opt out (see gpuweb#2076, still open, and gpuweb#2402).

The probe below reproduces exactly what a deep-zoom fragment shader does: take a double-single centre, add a per-pixel offset held in plain f32, and see how many distinct coordinates 32 adjacent pixels end up with. 32 means the zoom resolves per pixel. A smaller number is the width, in pixels, of the flat blocks that appear on screen.
